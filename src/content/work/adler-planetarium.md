summary: "A major website launch is already an integration problem. For Adler Planetarium, the challenge intensified when the final readiness window compressed — the question was no longer whether the work was complete, but whether the organization was actually ready to go live."
visibleProblem: "When the final readiness window tightened, more work had to happen in parallel and decisions had to happen faster. A traditional list of open tickets was not enough. The team needed to distinguish between defects that threatened a core visitor journey, issues that could be remediated quickly, and improvements that should not hold the launch."
hiddenSystemIssue: "The website could not be evaluated in isolation. The visitor journey crossed the site, ticketing system, analytics, content, and operational handoffs owned by different teams and vendors. There was no shared readiness model — only separate work streams with no common standard for what \"done\" meant across all of them."
stakes: "A public launch tied to visitor expectations, cross-system integration across design, content, technology, analytics, and ticketing — and an organization that needed shared evidence to make responsible go/no-go decisions under pressure."
operatingIntervention: "I led delivery and launch-readiness work across the website and its connected systems. I created a more explicit readiness structure around the final push: journey-based UAT rather than disconnected page review; shared severity definitions for triage; daily visibility into defects, decisions, and remediation; clear go-live criteria; decision tracking so issues did not repeatedly reopen; and coordination across design, development, analytics, ticketing, content, and Adler stakeholders."
whatChanged: "The launch succeeded. Post-launch work extended into QA, analytics and measurement planning, and building a clearer measurement foundation across GA4, GTM, the ticketing system, and campaign attribution — so the organization could move from simply having a new digital experience to understanding how people were actually moving through it."
leadershipLesson: "Launch confidence did not come from everything being perfect. It came from making the remaining risk visible enough to decide."
